How the indicative limit is calculated

  • Eligible receivables = open, non-duplicate invoices that are at most 60 days overdue, including VAT.
  • No single buyer may exceed the share set above; the excess is excluded.
  • Indicative limit = eligible receivables × advance rate. It supports a conversation with the bank; it is not a credit decision.

Assumptions behind the sample data

  • Fictional Jeddah beverage and water distributor selling to 12 hotels, supermarkets, cafés and caterers; 15% VAT; terms of 30, 45 or 60 days by buyer.
  • Seasonality: peaks in the summer heat (Jun–Aug), Ramadan and Eid (Feb–Mar) and Hajj season (May–Jun); slowest in winter; about 0.8% monthly growth.
  • Risk story: the largest buyer (a hotel group, about 19% of sales) leaves many Feb–Jun invoices unpaid, so overdue receivables build up. Two other buyers pay slowly.
  • Deliberate errors: 5 duplicated invoice numbers, 3 credit notes, 3 invoices with missing VAT, and an incomplete export for November 2025 (about 45% of invoices kept). The checks must find them.
  • Data is generated by scripts/generate_data.js (fixed seed 7319) into data/dataset.js, which this page loads. Bank deposits are built from paid invoices with noise: about 5% paid in cash (no deposit), 7% paid slightly short, deposits 1 day early or late, plus 25 unexplained deposits.
